    How to play

    1. The aim. Take turns dropping a disc into one of the seven columns. It falls to the lowest empty space. The first to line up four of their own discs wins: across, up and down, or on a slant.
    2. A full board. If all 42 spaces fill with no line of four, the game is a draw. Nobody scores, and in a match the next game starts.
    3. A match. The first to win the number of games set on the menu wins the match. With Take turns, the first move passes to the other side each game, draws included.
    4. Moving first. It matters more than it looks. With perfect play, whoever moves first and starts in the middle column always wins.
    5. The computer. Easy overlooks things. Medium never misses a win or a block, but it doesn't plan. Hard sees a few moves ahead and is sharp in a fight, but it can be outplayed. Guru thinks for a few seconds over each move, and plays perfectly as soon as it can work the rest of the game out.
    6. Undo. Against the computer, Undo takes back your last move and its reply, as many times as you like, even after you lose or draw. A win that used Undo still counts as a win, but it doesn't add to your streak or set a record, and the statistics list it separately.

    Wi-Fi: each of you plays on your own phone. One chooses Wi-Fi on the menu, types a name and taps Host a game; the other chooses Wi-Fi, taps Join a game and enters the four letters on the host's screen, or opens the link the host shares. The host's match length and hints settings apply to both. A phone that drops out comes back by itself, and while it is away the host can play for it.

    Tap a column to drop a disc. Tap anywhere to skip an animation or a celebration. On a keyboard: the arrow keys and Space, or the keys 1 to 7, drop a disc; Enter presses the main button, H asks for a hint, U undoes and M mutes the sound.
